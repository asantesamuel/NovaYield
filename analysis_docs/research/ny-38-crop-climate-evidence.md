# NY-38 Crop Evidence and NASA Climate Method

This specification distinguishes a measured NASA meteorological value, a calculated climate-exposure feature, and an agronomic claim about a crop or field. Only the first two can be established from the current data alone. The [NY-38 handoff](ny-38-research-handoff.md), [data contract](ny-38-data-contract.md), [NY-13 source specification](ny-13-nasa-power-input-specification.md), and [Product Owner decisions](../../novayield-research-and-product-decisions.md#6-finalized-product-decisions-and-provisional-assumptions) define its context. All crop-specific application to Ejura remains provisional under Decision 9.

This is the **documented native source grid** for the three meteorological variables checked at the public pilot points, not a field-scale measurement or proof that every returned parameter uses that grid. The sampled JSON has source names, parameter units/fill and a rounded point geometry but **no numeric grid size, cell ID/bounds or per-day source**. Historical ASCII labels mention an average 0.5° × 0.625° region; the recent ASCII parameter labels still say MERRA-2 while recent JSON lists GEOS-IT, so do not infer daily provenance from static ASCII labels. Retain the returned JSON metadata; where it does not report grid size, use `documented_source_grid` with its citation rather than pretending NASA returned per-request resolution metadata. [NASA's Daily, Monthly and Climatology services](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/) answer different questions. Daily values preserve rainfall distribution and stage windows; monthly aggregates can summarize seasons but cannot reconstruct the longest run of dry days; climatology supplies multi-year monthly aggregates but not the year-by-year variability needed for dry spells. Decision 15 accepts **1991–2020** as the reference because [WMO identifies it as the current 30-year climatological standard normal period](https://community.wmo.int/site/knowledge-hub/programmes-and-initiatives/climate-services/wmo-climatological-normals). This is a POWER **grid reference**, not a GMet normal, a future forecast, or a claim of crop suitability. A fixed reference-period/version must be saved with each result.

POWER parameters can originate in products with different native resolutions and update histories. Keep source, units, fill value, date coverage, time standard, retrieval time and available spatial metadata **per parameter**. Do not display a single invented resolution or private coordinate. NASA's [data-processing methods](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/processing/) and [source descriptions](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources_meteorology/) are the source for the distinction.

### Three separate time concepts

1. **Reported crop events** are actual inclusive planting/establishment-to-harvest/end dates on the owner's plot. They come from the farmer, not NASA or a calendar.
2. **Farm context** is the derived minimum-12-calendar-month historical window ending at the latest reported crop end. It may be longer when the reported events span longer. It is not a long-term climate normal.
3. **Climate reference** is the separate Decision 15-approved 1991–2020 Daily POWER series used to describe historical distributions for comparable future season/stage windows. It is not future weather. A future climate forecast is outside this ticket and the current MVP.

## 2. Existing approved indicators

For an inclusive period `W`, take one value per UTC day after checking the response's parameter unit and fill value. Let `valid_P(W)`, `valid_T(W)`, and `valid_Tmax(W)` be the valid dated values for each parameter. The existing NY-7 definitions are:

| Indicator | Exact calculation | Unit if response metadata confirms it | Missingness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Precipitation total | `P(W) = sum(PRECTOTCORR[d] for d in valid_P(W))` | mm per period from daily mm/day values | If zero valid days, unavailable. Otherwise report expected, valid and missing counts; partial sum remains explicitly partial, never a complete total. |
| Mean air temperature | `Tmean(W) = sum(T2M[d] for d in valid_T(W)) / count(valid_T(W))` | °C | Same count/coverage disclosure; no zero substitution. |
| Mean daily maximum air temperature | `Tmax_mean(W) = sum(T2M_MAX[d] for d in valid_Tmax(W)) / count(valid_Tmax(W))` | °C | Same disclosure; this is not the hottest single day. |

Decision 4 calls for all three when their metadata and coverage are usable, or the reliable two with a reason when one fails. It does not approve an invented percentage coverage cutoff. These indicators describe historical grid conditions, not field rain, soil moisture, effective rainfall, crop water need, or a temperature damage threshold. [NASA Daily documentation](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) and [NY-13](ny-13-nasa-power-input-specification.md) support their definitions.

### Other candidate features evaluated

The formulas below are **research candidates**, not approved crop scores. For a candidate's relevant calendar window `W`, a year `y`, and the complete Decision 15-approved [1991–2020 WMO-compatible reference](https://community.wmo.int/site/knowledge-hub/programmes-and-initiatives/climate-services/wmo-climatological-normals) at the same location/product/time standard, calculate one value per corresponding year before any reference statistic. Each used daily series must retain expected/valid/missing counts; incomplete windows are `not_assessed` for a rankable comparison unless a separate missing-data policy is reviewed.

| Feature | Exact candidate calculation and unit | What it can say | Why it does not yet change a crop rating |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seasonal rain amount | `RainTotal_y(W) = sum(PRECTOTCORR[d])` over every expected valid day; mm | Historical grid rainfall total for that window | It is not effective rainfall or crop water sufficiency; no crop-specific demand and soil/irrigation method is approved. |
| Wet-day count / distribution | `WetDays_y(W) = count(PRECTOTCORR[d] >= 1 mm)`; days, plus the ordered daily series | Describes frequency and spacing of meteorological rain days using the [CDD convention](https://climpact-sci.org/indices/) | One wet day can have very different amounts; no crop-stage response has been reviewed. |
| Rain anomaly | `RainAnomaly(W) = RainTotal_recent(W) - median_y(RainTotal_y(W))`; mm, for the **same calendar window** | Shows whether the observed recent window was wetter or drier than the grid reference median | A retrospective difference is not a future forecast or a crop-performance threshold. Undefined if recent/reference coverage is incomplete. |
| Mean-temperature anomaly | `TAnomaly(W) = Tmean_recent(W) - mean_y(Tmean_y(W))`; °C, same calendar window | Describes observed relative warmth in the recent historical period | No named variety/stage temperature-response rule or heat limit is approved. |
| Heat-exposure days | `HeatDays(W,crop) = count(T2M_MAX[d] > crop_stage_threshold_c)`; days | Could count source-defined threshold exceedances **if** a locally applicable crop/stage threshold exists | The threshold is currently unknown; do not use an arbitrary 30/35/40 °C cutoff. Return `not_assessed`. |

The 12-calendar-month farm context need not contain every historical season window, especially when the last crop ended long ago. A recent-window anomaly is calculated only for an actually covered matching period. The long-term reference is a different data product use from that recent context. No anomaly or heat count above is approved to alter Decision 13 scoring; only the narrower provisional dry-spell tie preference below is approved under Decision 15.

## 3. Approved provisional numeric feature: stage-window dry-spell exposure

The first approved provisional feature calculable from the **existing** rainfall variable uses the standard **consecutive dry days** (CDD) dry-day/run definition, adapted here to a sourced crop-stage window rather than an annual index. [Climpact/ETCCDI](https://climpact-sci.org/indices/) defines a dry day as precipitation **below 1 mm**. This 1 mm cutoff defines a meteorological index, **not** a threshold at which a crop suffers damage. The crop-stage application and narrow tie preference are NovaYield implementation choices under Decision 15, not claims that ETCCDI itself validates an agronomic decision.

For a sourced, inclusive crop growth-stage window (W), define (D_d=1) when valid daily `PRECTOTCORR_d < 1 mm`, otherwise (D_d=0). Then:

```text
CDD(W) = maximum length of a consecutive run of dates d in W for which D_d = 1
WetDays(W) = count of valid dates d in W for which PRECTOTCORR_d >= 1 mm
RainTotal(W) = sum of valid PRECTOTCORR_d in W, in mm
```

For a complete window with no dry days, CDD is measured **0 days**. If any expected day is absent, malformed or the provider fill value, CDD is `not_assessed` for ranking: the unseen day could join two runs. A visible lower bound may be labeled `partial_observed`, with expected/valid/missing counts, but may not stand in for full-window CDD. Negative precipitation other than the documented fill value is invalid, not zero. A synthetic seven-day test vector `[0, 0.2, 3, 0, 0, 0.4, 2]` mm/day yields runs of two and three dry days, so CDD is three; replacing any entry with a missing value makes the rankable CDD unavailable.

For a **future candidate event**, the crop, production system, local planting window, and water-sensitive stage's sourced start offset and duration must first be resolved from versioned evidence. The Decision 15 scheduler returns a **planting window**, not one exact planting day. It therefore cannot compute one exact stage CDD without further input or an arbitrary date choice. The approved conservative window-aware method enumerates every feasible planting day within the sourced window and every supported stage-offset/duration value, calculates the corresponding 1991–2020 CDD distribution for each, and retains the interval from the lowest to highest median. Keep the 30 per-year values in the pure calculation output for audit; persist the exposure interval, summary counts, exact source snapshot IDs, feature version, and stage/profile/calendar versions so it can be reproduced without copying every daily value into each result. The stage must fit the candidate's crop-specific event. A missing/incompatible reference year or an undefined stage offset makes the feature `not_assessed`; the strict method requires all 30 complete corresponding windows for each compared placement rather than inventing a coverage percentage. Define crop-year and leap-day alignment before assessment if a window crosses a year boundary or contains 29 February.

The same calculation may be made over a matching stage window in the derived recent farm context **only when that complete window exists there**. It describes a retrospective scenario. A last-year dry spell does not predict next season's dry spell. The 1991–2020 distribution is historical exposure, not a future rainfall prediction or a farm water balance.

### A concrete, reviewable decision effect

The sourced agronomic connection is **stage sensitivity**, not an invented water threshold: [FAO identifies maize flowering/grain filling, groundnut flowering/pod setting and rice head development/flowering as shortage-sensitive](https://www.fao.org/4/t7202e/t7202e05.htm); [FAO describes cassava's first three months as establishment-sensitive](https://www.fao.org/4/i2929e/i2929e.pdf). A named crop profile and dated stage are needed before its CDD is meaningful. Under Decision 15's provisional **same crop, same variety/system, same sensitive stage** comparison, two otherwise comparable planting windows can have different 1991–2020 dry-spell exposure intervals. Only a strict separation—one window's *highest* feasible median CDD is below the other's *lowest*—supports a provisional, source-labeled preference for the lower exposure. Overlapping intervals are incomparable, not a hidden tie-break. An artificial contract test could use one window with exposure interval `[3,4]` days and another `[7,8]` days; no such values are asserted for Ejura.

For a **rotation-level comparison** that can distinguish actual three-crop permutations, compare only options with the **same multiset of crop IDs, varieties, systems and assessed stages**. Match each crop occurrence across options; repeated identical crops need a deterministic occurrence pairing or remain incomparable. Option A has lower historical stage dry-spell exposure than B only if **every** matched crop/stage's A upper bound is strictly below B's lower bound. This is a partial order: equal or overlapping intervals, mixed advantages or missing stages yield no climate preference. Apply it only after Decision 13's hard constraints, selected-mode scoring and farmer weights produce an **equal total**; preserve the original score, annotate the explicit climate tie preference, and show the per-crop exposure trade-offs. Because NY-8 already generates permutations of the same crop set, this is a concrete way actual NASA numbers can alter the displayed order of eligible tied options without treating unrelated crops' dry-day counts as equivalent water demand. This is an approved **provisional** policy and may produce no preference when evidence is incomplete.

**Approval recorded:** The Product Owner accepted the matched-crop permutation preference, the all-feasible-days calculation, and the plot-availability input for NY-40 under [Decision 15](../../novayield-research-and-product-decisions.md#decision-15--ny-38-farm-and-climate-implementation-handoff). It may enter only after Decision 13's hard constraints and selected weighted criteria yield an equal total. Under Decision 9, expert review is deferred: use before local review remains visibly **provisional**, limited to adequately sourced crop/stage scopes, and subject to later agronomist/farmer validation. CDD alone cannot compare unrelated crops' water sufficiency or convert a crop into High/Moderate/Low. If the stage, calendar, crop system, common comparison basis, or NASA series is missing, return `not_assessed`; Decision 13's four-criterion or water/soil fallback and unranked behavior remains. Do not add an unexplained CDD point bonus or silently break an unsupported tie.

Temperature has a real future route into crop assessment through FAO reference evapotranspiration below. Until crop/stage-specific heat evidence and a reviewed mapping exist, `T2M` or `T2M_MAX` may be summarized or compared with a historical reference but must not be called a crop heat threshold or directly alter a crop rating.

## 4. Full crop-water method and missing inputs

A more general **cross-crop** water assessment would use the [FAO-56 Penman–Monteith reference evapotranspiration](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e06.htm), then a sourced crop coefficient for each stage:

```text
ET0_d = [0.408 Δ (Rn − G) + γ × 900/(T + 273) × u2 × (es − ea)]
        / [Δ + γ × (1 + 0.34 u2)]                    mm/day
ETc_d = Kc(crop, variety, system, stage, d) × ET0_d  mm/day
TAW   = 1000 × (θFC − θWP) × Zr                     mm
Dr_d  = Dr_(d−1) − (P_d − RO_d) − I_d − CR_d + ETc,adj_d + DP_d
```

Here `Rn`/`G` are radiation/soil heat flux, `T` air temperature, `u2` wind at 2 m, `es−ea` vapour-pressure deficit, `Δ` saturation-vapour-pressure slope, and `γ` the psychrometric constant. `Kc` is a sourced crop/stage coefficient; `θFC−θWP` and rooting depth `Zr` determine total available root-zone water; `Dr` tracks depletion, `RO` runoff, `I` infiltrated irrigation, `CR` capillary rise, and `DP` deep percolation. [FAO crop coefficients](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e0a.htm) and [FAO root-zone balance](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm) explain these terms and their assumptions.

The present `PRECTOTCORR,T2M,T2M_MAX` set lacks radiation, humidity/vapour pressure and wind needed for FAO-56 ET0. A successful API probe found candidate POWER variables `T2M_MIN`, `RH2M`, `WS2M`, `ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN`, `PS` and `T2MDEW`; the header combined MERRA-2 and solar-source `SYN1DEG`, so source/resolution must be checked per variable. `ET0` itself was not a valid requested parameter. Even with extra weather data, the current Farm does not contain measured field capacity/wilting point, rooted soil depth, initial water, runoff/infiltration, actual irrigation volume/timing, or validated variety/stage Kc. Farmer-reported broad texture and irrigation **access** do not supply those quantities. No present crop-water balance, effective-rainfall amount, water sufficiency, yield impact, or cross-crop numeric rating can be claimed from the current inputs. A future method must define how each term is sourced, estimated and validated; otherwise return `not_assessed`.

**Effective rainfall** must not be equated with `PRECTOTCORR` total. Rainfall can run off, evaporate, bypass roots or drain below the root zone; soil depth, structure, infiltration, antecedent water and management matter. A future simplified effective-rainfall model would need its own cited assumptions and local testing. Without that work, the CDD feature remains meteorological exposure only.

**Source confidence and pilot transferability:** Ghana MoFA/CSIR variety and regional guide records offer moderate-to-high confidence about their stated varieties and systems, but only low-to-moderate transferability to an unspecified Ejura plot. Ejura field studies provide stronger place context but still refer to particular sample years, farmers, varieties or intercrops. FAO global guidance is credible for method and broad sensitivity; transfer to the exact local farm is low until local variety/calendar/management review. Website publication dates for the CSIR catalog were not verified; its `Year` column is a **variety release year**, not the web page publication date. Retain an unknown publication date rather than substituting that year.

### Catalog coverage and intercropping

The [Ejura Municipal Assembly](https://esma.gov.gh/about-us/town-profile/) lists plantain and generic “beans,” and [MoFA's district profile](https://mofa.gov.gh/site/directorates/55-district-directorates/district-ashanti/164-ejura-sekyeredumase) includes plantain and guinea corn beyond the six recommendation candidates. A six-record history selector would prevent some farmers from recording truthful histories. Decision 15 permits sourced **history-only** identities in the same canonical catalog without expanding the recommendation candidates; free-text escape or silently translating “beans” to cowpea is not approved.

An [IITA Ghana forest–savanna example](https://biblio.iita.org/documents/S17InbkKuyperIntercroppingNothomNodev.pdf-a2205c618fc1ecce13d043c879e43b6a.pdf) documents yam, cassava and maize occupying a plot concurrently, and the Ejura groundnut study tests intercrops. A strict ordered sole-crop history must therefore explain that overlapping/intercropped events are `needs_review` or unsupported in this MVP; it cannot pretend the farm was sequentially monocropped.

[Decision 8](../../novayield-research-and-product-decisions.md#decision-8--nasa-snapshot-refresh-and-freshness) calls for a refresh after NASA's approximately 2–3-day publication latency and reuse of older historical requests. [NASA service guidance](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/) describes replacement of quick-look meteorology with climate-quality data within about 2–3 months, while [NASA's source methods](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources_meteorology/) say within a few months and note subsequent MERRA-2 updates. That is **not a guaranteed replacement date**. Decision 15 accepts a second, source-aware check for snapshots containing GEOS-IT dates: monitor provider availability/source and refresh after the record is superseded, rather than applying a fixed TTL to all historical data. NY-6 should preserve a valid cached response if either refresh fails, record which source supplied it, and refresh on a documented provider correction. The operational check intervals are configurable, not guaranteed provider deadlines.

## 7. Review questions and tests for the method

1. **Agronomist/extension:** Which named varieties and community windows are suitable for each pilot location? What are their exact planting-to-harvest and stage boundaries? Is the sourced FAO sensitive stage relevant under local management? How should intercrops and cassava's long occupancy be handled?
2. **Farmer/extension:** Can farmers report actual event dates, variety, rice ecology, plot occupancy, soil texture source and irrigation access reliably? What information is typically unknown?
3. **Product scope after Decision 15:** The same-crop CDD tie preference and visibly provisional status are approved only for fully sourced, comparable stages and calendars; missing or conflicting evidence remains unranked/`needs_review`. Whether a full FAO-56 water method belongs in later scope remains a future Product Owner question once its additional inputs are available.
4. **Technical/data:** Verify parameter-specific resolution and metadata at each public point, compare POWER rainfall to an appropriate local gauge if accessible, document how a source replacement changes existing snapshots, and test missing/partial/mixed-source data. Do not use a hard numeric coverage cutoff without a reviewed basis.

Minimum deterministic feature cases: all-wet window (`CDD=0`), two dry runs, a dry run crossing month/year boundary, one fill value splitting a possible run (`not_assessed`), negative malformed precipitation, variable source or unit mismatch, incomplete reference year, leap-day/cross-year calendar ambiguity, identical inputs/version reproducing the same feature, and two synthetic matched-crop permutations whose NASA values create strict climate dominance while their Decision 13 totals tie. Also test overlapping exposure intervals, mixed crop advantages, different crop multisets and absent stage evidence preserving the tie or returning `not_assessed`. Decision 15 approves that narrow conditional tie preference in production; the synthetic vectors verify mechanics, not local agronomic evidence.
