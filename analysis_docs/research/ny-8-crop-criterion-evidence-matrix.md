# NY-8 Provisional Crop-by-Criterion Evidence Matrix

## Purpose and evidence status

This matrix maps public research to the six approved MVP crops. Under Product
Owner Decision 13 (4 November 2026), all four criteria are active: water
resilience, soil-health potential, yield-stability proxy, and labour feasibility.
Farmer-selected priorities may change ordering when every criterion is
comparable. The matrix records
what sources can support for a crop or particular crop relationship, how
confidently evidence transfers to provisional Ejura-Sekyedumase, and what
remains unknown.

**This is not a score table.** A source can support a crop trait without
supporting a rating for every rotation containing that crop. Confidence below
describes evidence strength and transferability, not confidence that a crop
will perform a particular way on a user's farm. Published findings remain
provisional for NovaYield until local review. Keep `not_assessed` where the
available evidence does not provide a comparable rating basis.

The underlying area profile is not a real farm. It has no field soil test,
residue-management record, crop variety, irrigation measurement, or documented
three-season plot history. The historical NASA
periods in the demo profile are environmental-analysis windows, not future
planting slots. See the [NY-9 season-calendar handoff](ny-9-pilot-season-calendar-evidence.md).

### Confidence labels

- **Source confidence**: how directly and credibly the source supports the
  stated observation (for example, a measured field trial versus a broad guide).
- **Pilot transferability**: how closely the source's place, crop variety,
  production system, management, and season match the MVP profile.
- “High” source confidence never means locally validated for NovaYield. A low
  transferability label prevents the observation from being treated as a local
  hard rule or a farm-specific result.

## Water resilience

NASA seasonal rainfall and temperature are environmental context. Crop water
need or drought sensitivity alone does not establish field water availability,
water sufficiency, or the response of a particular variety in a particular
future season.

| Crop | Cited evidence and provisional interpretation | Confidence and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maize** | The current provisional class is Low relative water-stress resilience, using FAO's indicative 500–800 mm crop-water need and medium-high drought sensitivity. | Moderate source confidence for broad crop context; low-to-moderate pilot transferability. Variety, planting window, crop duration, soil, and water access are not represented. [FAO crop water needs](https://www.fao.org/4/S2022E/s2022e07.htm). |
| **Cowpea** | The current provisional class is Moderate, Low confidence. A Ghana Tono irrigation study supplies water-use context; Ghana trials show drought response varies by genotype and environment. | The irrigated Upper East site and tested genotypes may not represent an unspecified Ejura rain-fed farm/cultivar. [Tono water-use study](https://cspace.csirgh.com/items/show/1466); [Ghana cowpea drought variation study](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/6ca7b2cc-9533-4d3b-9922-cf4589f3a914). |
| **Groundnut** | The current provisional class is High relative water-stress resilience, Low confidence, from FAO's indicative 500–700 mm need and low-medium drought sensitivity. | Generic FAO range; the trial's accession, year, and location effects cannot be assigned to an unspecified variety. [FAO crop water needs](https://www.fao.org/4/S2022E/s2022e07.htm); [Northern Ghana groundnut trial](https://cspace.csirgh.com/items/show/139). |
| **Cassava** | The current provisional class is Moderate, Moderate confidence: FAO describes drought tolerance after establishment and sensitivity during the first three months. | Variety, planting date, early-season soil moisture, and management are unknown. [FAO Save and Grow: Cassava](https://www.fao.org/4/i3278e/i3278e.pdf). |
| **Yam** | The current provisional class is Low, Low confidence from a broad West African review describing long crop duration and need for sufficient, well-distributed moisture. | Broad regional evidence, not an Ejura cultivar/calendar estimate. [West African yam review](https://doi.org/10.5772/intechopen.106504). |
| **Rice** | Not assessed. FAO paddy figures and Tono irrigation results must not be generalized while the MVP crop record lacks rice ecology. | Upland, lowland, and irrigated systems differ; production system and water access are unknown. [FAO crop water needs](https://www.fao.org/4/S2022E/s2022e07.htm); [Ghana rice strategy](https://www.jica.go.jp/english/our_work/thematic_issues/agricultural/pdf/ghana_en.pdf). |

**Scoring implication:** NY-8 v4 carries these cited, coarse crop classes into the active water criterion. A sequence takes the least water-resilient rated crop as a conservative bottleneck; any sequence containing an unassessed crop (currently rice without an identified ecology) has water `not_assessed` and remains unranked. The classes do not estimate actual crop water requirements or farm water sufficiency. NASA historical precipitation/temperature is attached as context and does not change them. Local calendar, variety, soil, irrigation, and field validation remain outstanding.

## Soil-health potential

Published Ghana rotation trials support specific legume/residue effects, especially
on a following maize crop. They do not support a universal legume bonus, a
measured soil-health claim, or an assumption that residues are retained.

| Crop | Cited evidence and provisional interpretation | Confidence and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maize** | In Ghana experiments, maize following particular cowpea/groundnut systems or incorporated legume residues had improved nitrogen/yield outcomes in the studied treatments. This is evidence about a specific preceding-legume relationship, not maize's stand-alone soil-health potential. | Moderate source confidence for the treatment studied; low pilot transferability across varieties, soils, residue management, and the proposed three-event sequences. [Kermah et al. 2019](https://pure.ug.edu.gh/en/publications/legume-maize-rotation-or-relay-options-for-ecological-intensifica-2/); [Nyalemegbe et al. 2012](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/d346fb22-09de-4507-9c62-3d1c55e91c7e). |
| **Cowpea** | Ghana field studies measured variety-specific biomass/nitrogen and succeeding-maize effects; results depend on variety, fertility, and whether residues are returned. A cowpea crop label alone does not establish a soil benefit. | Moderate source confidence for specific varieties/treatments; low transferability without cultivar and residue-management inputs. [Nyalemegbe et al. 2012](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/d346fb22-09de-4507-9c62-3d1c55e91c7e); [Kermah et al. 2019](https://pure.ug.edu.gh/en/publications/legume-maize-rotation-or-relay-options-for-ecological-intensifica-2/). |
| **Groundnut** | Groundnut is included in Ghana legume–maize rotation trials; biomass/residue treatments and subsequent maize responses were measured. This supports a possible, management-dependent rotation relationship, not an unconditional soil-health rating. | Moderate source confidence for named study systems; low transferability to an unknown farm and unrecorded residue handling. [Nyalemegbe et al. 2012](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/d346fb22-09de-4507-9c62-3d1c55e91c7e); [Kermah et al. 2019](https://pure.ug.edu.gh/en/publications/legume-maize-rotation-or-relay-options-for-ecological-intensifica-2/). |
| **Cassava** | The reviewed pilot sources do not provide a comparable, crop-specific positive soil-health effect for cassava rotations. | Not assessed. Soil texture, fertility, erosion, harvest practice, and residue/organic-input management are unknown. |
| **Yam** | Ghana research has studied yam with pigeonpea and soil/resource outcomes, but pigeonpea is not in the MVP candidate library and the experiment does not establish a sole-yam rotation rating. | Low transferability to the candidate sequences; do not infer a yam benefit from a different companion crop. [Pigeonpea–yam Ghana study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0570178322000069). |
| **Rice** | Rice soil outcomes depend on production system and water management; the farm's upland/lowland/irrigated system is not recorded, and the reviewed sources do not provide a comparable NovaYield rotation value. | Not assessed. The production-system input must be identified before applying system-specific evidence. [Ghana rice strategy](https://www.jica.go.jp/english/our_work/thematic_issues/agricultural/pdf/ghana_en.pdf). |

**Scoring implication:** Soil health is an active criterion, but the current profile has no field soil test or residue history. The assessor therefore leaves soil `not_assessed`; this prevents four-criterion scoring and omits soil from the fallback, which uses water alone. Sequence-specific Ghana findings may support future assessments only when management/residue context is present and evidence is comparable across every option. They are not measured soil outcomes.

## Yield-stability proxy (active criterion; currently not assessed)

Yield stability is not the same as average yield, a yield response to weather,
or crop presence in a district. Stability studies are often variety- and
environment-specific; these results cannot be assigned to an unspecified crop
record or combined into a common six-crop scale without a documented method.

| Crop | Cited evidence and provisional interpretation | Confidence and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maize** | An Ashanti study relates annual maize yield to climate variables, and the Ejura maize-failure study documents one severe local production failure. These support climate sensitivity context, not multi-environment stability for each candidate rotation. | Moderate source confidence for the reported context; low confidence as a comparative stability rating. The local failure is not a complete rotation trial. [Ashanti climate/yield study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/2022/9077383); [Ejura 2020 maize-failure study](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/6/3514). |
| **Cowpea** | Ghana multi-location trials report genotype-by-environment effects and stability differences among tested lines. This establishes that variety and location matter; it does not give a score for an unspecified cowpea in an Ejura rotation. | Moderate-to-high source confidence for tested lines/environments; low transferability to an unspecified variety and sequence. [Ghana cowpea stability study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11759354/). |
| **Groundnut** | Northern Ghana multi-location trials found accession, year, location, and interaction effects on yield stability. Some accessions adapted differently to marginal and favourable environments. | Moderate source confidence for tested accessions; low transferability to Ejura and an unspecified variety. [Northern Ghana groundnut stability trial](https://cspace.csirgh.com/items/show/139). |
| **Cassava** | The Ashanti study analyzes annual cassava yields against climate variables. It does not estimate stability across crop varieties or rotation sequences. | Moderate climate-response evidence; low confidence for a yield-stability rating. [Ashanti climate/yield study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/2022/9077383). |
| **Yam** | The Ashanti study analyzes annual yam yield against climate variables, while local yam adaptation research reports farmers changing planting times. Neither compares the candidate rotations' stability. | Moderate local climate-context evidence; low confidence as a stability rating. [Ashanti climate/yield study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1155/2022/9077383); [Ejura yam adaptation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10008982/). |
| **Rice** | A northern Ghana climate study includes rice yields and finds climate effects differ across crops. It is useful evidence that crop-specific responses matter, but it does not establish an Ejura rice stability value. | Moderate source confidence for its northern Ghana study area; low pilot transferability and no matching production-system/variety information. [Northern Ghana climate/yield study](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/189376/). |

**Current product treatment:** Yield stability is active under Decision 13, but
the reviewed studies do not provide comparable ratings for the candidate
three-event sequences or unspecified varieties. Keep it `not_assessed` until a
common evidence basis is established; the ranking policy then uses the defined
water/soil fallback rather than withholding the overall rank solely for this
missing criterion.

## Labour feasibility (active criterion; currently not assessed)

Published work can describe crop-operation labor demand. **Feasibility** also
requires knowing the farm's available household/hired labor, machinery or
service access, operation timing, and area. The current Farm profile does not
capture those inputs.

| Crop | Cited evidence and provisional interpretation | Confidence and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maize** | Ghana labor studies report operation demand across farming systems; a northern Ghana field paper tabulates operation days for maize alongside cowpea and groundnut. | Moderate source confidence for the stated systems and operations; low confidence for feasibility on this farm. Studies are dated/system-specific and not a user labor survey. [IFPRI Ghana labor study](https://hdl.handle.net/10568/154236); [Kermah et al. 2017](https://biblio.iita.org/documents/U17ArtKermahMaizegrainInthomDev.pdf-445c35e8e1f6327f3454293ca87e73c6.pdf). |
| **Cowpea** | Northern Ghana field literature lists separate sowing, weeding, harvest, and threshing labor estimates. | Moderate evidence for a study-specific operation budget; low pilot transferability and no farm capacity data. [Kermah et al. 2017](https://biblio.iita.org/documents/U17ArtKermahMaizegrainInthomDev.pdf-445c35e8e1f6327f3454293ca87e73c6.pdf). |
| **Groundnut** | Northern Ghana operation budgets and Ghana production-package trials show that labor varies by operation, variety, location, and management package. | Moderate evidence for those packages; low confidence for a generic score. Kumasi results are relevant context but do not measure this farm's labor or its preferred practice. [Kermah et al. 2017](https://biblio.iita.org/documents/U17ArtKermahMaizegrainInthomDev.pdf-445c35e8e1f6327f3454293ca87e73c6.pdf); [Ghana peanut labor proceedings](https://apresinc.com/images/Annual_Meeting_Proceedings/2022_APRES_Proceedings_Final.pdf). |
| **Cassava** | IFPRI reports greater labor demand than maize in the studied systems, associated with mound construction and a longer crop period requiring more weeding. | Moderate source confidence for those production systems; low farm-level feasibility confidence. Management, mechanization, herbicides, and crop duration change labor demand. [IFPRI Ghana labor study](https://hdl.handle.net/10568/154236). |
| **Yam** | IFPRI reports system-dependent labor demand, with mound construction a substantial land-preparation task; yam generally demanded more labor than maize and rice but less than cassava in the cited systems. | Moderate source confidence for the studied systems; low farm-level feasibility confidence. Variety, mound method, plot, labor availability, and mechanization matter. [IFPRI Ghana labor study](https://hdl.handle.net/10568/154236). |
| **Rice** | IFPRI describes rice labor across systems and notes substantial land-preparation/harvesting labor; rice ecology and establishment/harvest methods differ. | Moderate source confidence for the studied systems; low pilot transferability until the farm's upland/lowland/irrigated system and labor capacity are known. [IFPRI Ghana labor study](https://hdl.handle.net/10568/154236); [Ghana rice strategy](https://www.jica.go.jp/english/our_work/thematic_issues/agricultural/pdf/ghana_en.pdf). |

**Current product treatment:** Labour feasibility is active under Decision 13,
but the current Farm profile lacks labour availability, plot area, operation
timing, and machinery/service access. Study-specific operation figures cannot
stand in for those farm inputs. Keep the criterion `not_assessed`; this missing
criterion prevents four-criterion scoring, so the defined water/soil fallback
is used instead.

## Cross-criterion ranking readiness

The provisional rule proposal is documented in
[NY-8 Provisional Agronomic Rotation Rules](ny-8-provisional-rotation-rules.md).

| Criterion | What can be supported now | What blocks comparable option ratings | Current profile status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water resilience | Coarse literature-informed crop classes, with confidence/source caveats. | Unknown rice ecology, local varieties, dates, field water access, and local validation; unassessed water stays out of rank. | Some provisional crop ratings; not farm-specific water balance |
| Soil-health potential | Specific legume/residue effects on following maize under measured treatments. | Plot soil/residue/fertility state and equivalent sequence evidence; no universal legume bonus. | `not_assessed`; omitted from fallback, which uses water alone |
| Yield-stability proxy | Crop- or variety-specific climate/yield and multi-environment studies. | No comparable ratings for candidate three-event sequences or unspecified varieties. | `not_assessed`; selects fallback mode |
| Labour feasibility | Study-specific operation estimates for some systems. | Farm labour capacity, area, operation timing, and machinery/service access are absent. | `not_assessed`; selects fallback mode |

Under Product Owner Decision 13, use all four criteria when every eligible
alternative has comparable evidence. Otherwise rank the alternatives with
comparable water evidence, adding soil only when it is assessed and comparable
across that full subset. Normalize farmer priorities over the criteria used;
missing criteria are not neutral ratings and receive no weight. If water is
unassessed for an option, leave that option unranked. Save each citation, scope,
confidence, assumption, rating, and comparison basis with the result.
Calendar/suitability warnings remain visible, and the output remains provisional
until local review.

## Date-input contract for NY-9

Keep these concepts separate in both the request schema and result provenance:

1. **Past farmer-reported seasons**: optional crop history plus an inclusive
   historical NASA analysis period for each reported season. Only these periods
   are sent to NY-6/NASA POWER. The report supports a baseline only when all
   three crops are supplied for the same plot.
2. **Future recommendation seasons**: three distinct, chronological planting
   slots for NY-8 candidate events. These are not NASA request windows. Until a
   sourced crop/community calendar exists, the API must retain an unknown or
   provisional calendar status, show a warning, and must not claim crop
   eligibility or validated timing. A provisional comparison is not a calendar
   validation.

Do not reuse the past analysis dates as future planting-event dates. A future
slot without a reviewed calendar is still review-needed and can block ranking.
